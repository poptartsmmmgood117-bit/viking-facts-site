IV · Chronicle

The Viking Age

From the first raid to the last stand: AD 793 to 1066.

AD 793

Lindisfarne

Norse raiders strike the monastery at Lindisfarne. The shock across Christian Europe marks the traditional start of the Viking Age.

AD 841

Dublin

Vikings build a fortified base on the Liffey, the beginning of the city of Dublin.

AD 865

The Great Heathen Army

A massive Viking force lands in England and topples kingdom after kingdom. Only Alfred the Great's Wessex holds.

c. AD 874

Iceland

Norse settlers reach Iceland. In 930 they found the Althing, one of the oldest parliaments on Earth.

AD 911

Normandy

The warlord Rollo is granted land in France. His descendants, the Normans, will conquer England.

c. AD 1000

Vinland

Leif Erikson reaches North America, nearly 500 years before Columbus. The site at L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland, proves it.

AD 1066

Stamford Bridge

Harald Hardrada falls invading England. His death traditionally closes the Viking Age.

V · Corrections

Myth vs. Fact

Pop culture loves Vikings, and gets a lot of them wrong.

Myth

Vikings wore horned helmets.

Fact

No Viking-Age horned helmet has ever been found. The look comes from 1800s costume design for Wagner's operas.

Myth

"Viking" was the name of a people.

Fact

"Going a-viking" meant going raiding overseas. Most Norse people were farmers who never raided at all.

Myth

Vikings were filthy.

Fact

Archaeologists find combs, tweezers and ear spoons everywhere. English writers complained Vikings bathed every Saturday.

Myth

Vikings drank from skulls.

Fact

A mistranslation of an old poem. They drank from horns and cups.

Myth

Only men were warriors.

Fact

DNA showed a famous warrior grave at Birka, Sweden, belonged to a woman, buried with weapons and a strategy game.

Myth

Vikings only raided.

Fact

They traded as far as Baghdad. Thousands of Arabic silver coins lie buried in Scandinavian hoards.
